About

I am a PhD candidate in Political and Social Sciences at Universitat Pompeu Fabra and the Institut Barcelona d’Estudis Internacionals (IBEI), supervised by Aina Gallego, and an FI (Joan Oró) predoctoral fellow of the Generalitat de Catalunya.

My research studies the political economy of technological change: how automation and artificial intelligence reshape work, and when those shocks do or do not translate into voting, beliefs, and policy. I combine causal inference designs with computational methods, including LLM-based text classification with human validation and large-scale embedding pipelines.

Before the PhD, I worked as a data scientist at Paysafe and as a research associate at UC Riverside’s Center for Economic Forecasting and Development. In 2026 I was a visiting researcher at the University of Southern California, and I am a member of the OpenAI Economic Research Exchange research team.

Working Papers

Mid-Century Modern: Analyzing the Electoral Effects of Technological Change from the Post-War U.S. to Now
Presented at EPSA 2025 and EGN 2026
Abstract

Net automation and augmentation exposure across U.S. commuting zones (1952–2024) strongly predicts anti-incumbent voting in the post-war era, but the relationship collapses to zero in the modern period despite continued worker displacement. I develop a political translation framework and use LLM-based classification of party platforms to test observability and attribution channels.

Work in Progress

Through the Ad Glass: AI Exposure, Labor Markets, and Political Behavior in Switzerland
with Thomas Kurer
Description

We build a multi-stage NLP pipeline over 14.7 million Swiss job ads, combining LLM extraction with hand-validated evaluation and semantic similarity matching to O*NET tasks, to produce firm-occupation-year measures of AI exposure. Linking these to the Swiss Household Panel, we estimate effects on voting, economic outcomes, and political beliefs.

When Does Lobbying Shape AI Legislation? Evidence from the U.S. and EU
Description

Tracks lobbying around specific provisions across the legislative calendar to identify when advocacy is associated with changes in legislative text.
