01About

I am a PhD candidate at Universitat Pompeu Fabra and IBEI, supervised by Aina Gallego, and an FI (Joan Oró) predoctoral fellow. My dissertation examines the contestation and political effects of technological change, combining causal inference designs with computational methods such as LLM-based text classification with human validation and large-scale embedding pipelines.

In 2026 I was a visiting researcher at the University of Southern California, and I am a member of the OpenAI Economic Research Exchange research team. Before the PhD, I worked as a data scientist at Paysafe and as a research associate at UC Riverside’s Center for Economic Forecasting and Development.

02Research

  1. Mid-Century Modern: Analyzing the Electoral Effects of Technological Change from the Post-War U.S. to Now

    Working paperPresented at EPSA 2025 and EGN 2026

    Net automation and augmentation exposure across U.S. commuting zones (1952–2024) strongly predicts anti-incumbent voting in the post-war era, but the relationship collapses to zero in the modern period despite continued worker displacement. I develop a political translation framework and use LLM-based classification of party platforms to test observability and attribution channels.

  2. Through the Ad Glass: AI Exposure, Labor Markets, and Political Behavior in Switzerland

    In progresswith Thomas Kurer

    Firm-occupation-year AI exposure measures built from 14.7 million Swiss job ads, linked to the Swiss Household Panel to estimate effects on voting, economic outcomes, and political beliefs.

  3. When Does Lobbying Shape AI Legislation? Evidence from the U.S. and EU

    In progress

    Tracks lobbying around specific provisions across the legislative calendar to identify when advocacy is associated with changes in legislative text.

  4. Collective Bargaining, Business AI Use, and Worker Adjustment under Generative AI

    In progresswith Valentina González-Rostani and Paolo Agnolin
